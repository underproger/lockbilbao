163	How to prove the car is yours
164	<p>You need a photo ID — DNI, NIE or passport — and the vehicle registration certificate (<span lang="es">permiso de circulación</span>) or the technical inspection card (<span lang="es">ficha técnica</span>). The owner’s name or the registration number on the documents must match.</p>
165	What to do if the documents are inside the car
166	<p>This happens a lot: car documents are usually kept in the glovebox. Let the dispatcher know — the locksmith will open the car and check the documents straight after opening, before finishing the job.</p>
167	If the car is rented or a company car
168	<p>For a rental car, call the rental company first: many have their own assistance service. For a company car you will need confirmation from the company that you are its driver — for example, a written authorisation (<span lang="es">autorización</span>).</p>
169	Why a locksmith may refuse the job
170	<p>If your right to use the car cannot be confirmed with documents or in any other way, the locksmith may decline to open it. This protects owners: a stranger cannot call a locksmith to someone else’s car.</p>
171	When to call 112 instead of a locksmith
172	<p>If a child, an elderly person or an animal is left in a locked car, especially in hot weather, call the emergency services on 112 immediately. Don’t wait for the locksmith if there is a health risk.</p>

198	What to do if your keys are locked in the car?
199	A locked car with the keys inside is a nuisance, but it’s usually sorted in a single locksmith visit. Here’s what to check yourself, what not to do and how to prepare for the specialist’s arrival.
200	<h3>Keys left in the car: what to check first?</h3>
201	<p>Walk around the car and try all the doors and the boot: sometimes one door stays unlocked. If you have a spare key — at home, with relatives or colleagues — it can be quicker to bring it than to wait for a locksmith.</p>
202	<p>Don’t try to open the door with a wire, a ruler or a screwdriver: it’s easy to damage the seal, the lock linkages and the window motor wiring. Repairs after such an attempt cost more than the opening. See the <a href="#prices">price table</a> for guide prices.</p>
203	<h3>Why won’t the car open with the remote?</h3>
204	<p>Most often the remote’s battery is flat: the buttons stop working even though the key itself is fine. Many remotes hide a mechanical key — you can slide it out and open the driver’s door through the cylinder, if there is one.</p>
205	<p>If neither the remote nor the cylinder responds, the cause may be a flat car battery or a faulty door lock. Call a locksmith: based on your description, he’ll tell you what to check.</p>
206	<h3>Can a car be opened without damage?</h3>
207	<p>In most cases, yes. Special automotive tools make it possible to open the door without breaking a window or drilling the cylinder. It’s important to give the make, model and year straight away: the method depends on them.</p>
208	<p>If the door lock is broken or jammed, the locksmith will explain in advance what can be done and tell you the price. Find out more in the <a href="#no-damage">opening without damage</a> section.</p>
209	<h3>What to do if a key breaks in a car door lock?</h3>
210	<p>Don’t try to get the broken piece out with another key, a hairpin or glue: it’s easy to push it further in. The locksmith will extract it with a special tool and check the cylinder. Find out more about <a href="{url:svc-extraccion}">broken key extraction</a>.</p>
211	<h3>What to have ready before the locksmith arrives?</h3>
212	<p>The exact address or a landmark where the car is, its make, model, year and colour. If the car is in an underground car park, tell us how to get in. Have a photo ID ready — DNI, NIE or passport. If the car documents are inside, the locksmith will check them straight after opening. See which documents are accepted in the <a href="#documents">documents</a> section.</p>
