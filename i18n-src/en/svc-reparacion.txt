93	Not every fault calls for a replacement. The locksmith finds the cause first and suggests the solution that costs less and lasts longer.
94	When cleaning and lubrication are enough
95	<p>If the key is hard to turn because of dirt or dust, the mechanism is cleaned and treated with a dry lubricant. Oil and multi-purpose sprays are not suitable for cylinders: over time they collect dust.</p>
96	When the door is to blame, not the lock
97	<p>Over time a door drops, and the bolt starts to hit the strike plate. The hinges or strike plate are then adjusted — the lock itself is fine.</p>
98	When individual parts are replaced
99	<p>A broken spring, a worn latch or a loose handle can be replaced on its own — without replacing the whole lock.</p>

196	Good to know
197	Why do door locks break, and how can you make them last longer?
198	A lock rarely breaks without warning: it usually gives signs for weeks or months. Here’s what to look out for, what you can do yourself and when it’s time to call a locksmith.
199	<h3>Why is the key hard to turn in the lock?</h3>
200	<p>The most common causes are dirt and dust in the cylinder, a worn key or pins, and a misaligned door. In Bilbao, damp adds to this: in older buildings and in locks exposed to the weather, the mechanism oxidises faster.</p>
201	<p>Don’t use oil or multi-purpose sprays — they help for a couple of weeks and then collect dust. Cylinders need a graphite- or PTFE-based dry lubricant. If that doesn’t help, the lock needs a diagnosis.</p>
202	<h3>Why does the door take effort to close?</h3>
203	<p>Most often the door, not the lock, is the problem: it drops on its hinges or warps with damp, and the bolt starts to hit the strike plate. Forcing a door like this wears the lock out quickly.</p>
204	<p>The locksmith will adjust the hinges or reposition the strike plate — it’s inexpensive and usually quick. Find out more in the section <a href="#repair-or-replace">on repair and replacement</a>.</p>
205	<h3>When is it better to repair a lock, and when to replace it?</h3>
206	<p>A repair makes sense if the cause is dirt, adjustment or a single broken part. Replacement is needed if the cylinder is badly worn, the keys are lost or the lock has been discontinued and parts are no longer available.</p>
207	<p>If you’ve been meaning to improve your door’s security, a breakdown is a good moment to fit an anti-bump, anti-drill cylinder. Find out more about <a href="{url:svc-cambio}">lock and cylinder replacement</a>.</p>
208	<h3>What should I do if the lock has jammed and the door won’t open?</h3>
209	<p>Don’t try to turn the key with pliers — it may snap inside. Call a locksmith: they’ll open the door and repair or replace the lock in the same visit. Find out more about <a href="{url:svc-puertas}">emergency flat opening</a>.</p>
210	<h3>How can I make a lock last longer?</h3>
211	<p>Once a year, clean the cylinder and treat it with a dry lubricant, don’t hang a heavy bunch of keys from the key in the lock and don’t slam the door. If the key starts to turn harder, don’t wait for the lock to jam: an early repair costs less.</p>
