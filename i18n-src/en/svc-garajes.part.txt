198	What to do if your garage won’t open?
199	Garage doors usually fail at the worst moment — just when you need to drive off. Here’s what you can check yourself, what not to do and when it’s time to call a locksmith.
200	<h3>Why won’t the key turn in the garage lock?</h3>
201	<p>Garage and shutter locks are outdoors or in damp basements, so they suffer more from dirt, moisture and rust. The cylinder clogs up, and the bolt can seize to the strike plate.</p>
202	<p>Don’t use force or try to turn the key with pliers: a broken key will make opening harder. If the lock is just sticking, a <a href="{url:svc-reparacion}">lock repair</a> will help; if the key has already broken, you need <a href="{url:svc-extraccion}">broken key extraction</a>.</p>
203	<h3>What to do if the door doesn’t respond to the remote?</h3>
204	<p>First check the remote’s battery and whether there’s power in the garage. If the motor isn’t working, most doors have a manual release — usually a cord or lever on the motor inside the garage, and on some models a lock outside.</p>
205	<p>If you can’t reach the release, the locksmith will help you open the door manually. Repairing the motor and remotes is a job for door automation specialists.</p>
206	<h3>Can a metal shutter be opened without damage?</h3>
207	<p>Usually yes: if the problem is the lock, it is opened or replaced without touching the shutter. It’s harder if the shutter is twisted or has come off its tracks — then the locksmith will assess whether it can be fixed on the spot. Find out more in the <a href="#no-damage">opening without damage</a> section.</p>
208	<h3>Should the garage lock be changed after losing the keys?</h3>
209	<p>If the keys were lost together with documents or a key ring that could lead someone to your garage, it’s best to change the cylinder or padlock. It’s quick and inexpensive, and the old keys will no longer work. Find out more about <a href="{url:svc-cambio}">lock and cylinder replacement</a>.</p>
