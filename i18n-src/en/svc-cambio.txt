92	How to choose a new cylinder for your door
93	A good cylinder must be the right size and protect against common break-in methods. The locksmith will help you choose one to suit your door and budget.
94	Size — measured on site
95	<p>A cylinder is measured from the central fixing screw to each end: for example, 30×30 or 30×40 mm. A cylinder that’s too long sticks out of the door and is easier to snap.</p>
96	Anti-bump and anti-drill protection
97	<p>Standard cylinders are vulnerable to bump keys and drills. Security cylinders (<span lang="es">antibumping</span>, <span lang="es">antitaladro</span>) are specially reinforced against these methods.</p>
98	Anti-snap and anti-extraction protection
99	<p>An anti-snap cylinder (<span lang="es">antirrotura</span>) and a security escutcheon (<span lang="es">escudo</span>) stop the cylinder from being snapped and pulled out of the door.</p>
100	Keys with an owner’s card
101	<p>Copies of keys for a high-security cylinder are only cut on presentation of the owner’s card (<span lang="es">tarjeta de propiedad</span>). That way nobody can make a duplicate without your knowledge.</p>

197	Good to know
198	How do you know it’s time to replace a lock or cylinder?
199	The cylinder is the most vulnerable part of a door: it’s where burglars most often try to force the lock, and it’s also the first part to wear out. Here’s when it’s worth replacing it and how to choose a new one.
200	<h3>What’s the difference between replacing the cylinder and replacing the lock?</h3>
201	<p>The cylinder (<span lang="es">bombín</span>) is the removable part of the lock that the key goes into. It can be replaced without removing the whole lock — faster and cheaper. The whole lock is replaced if the case or bolts are broken, or if you want to switch to another system, such as a multi-point lock.</p>
202	<h3>When must the cylinder be replaced?</h3>
203	<p>After losing your keys if your address may have gone missing with them; after moving into a flat you’ve bought or rented; when former occupants, employees or tenants still have keys; after a break-in attempt. In all these cases, a new cylinder makes the old keys useless.</p>
204	<p>If your keys are lost and the door is locked, the locksmith opens it first and then fits a new cylinder straight away. Find out more about <a href="{url:svc-puertas}">emergency flat opening</a>.</p>
205	<h3>What is bumping, and how can you protect against it?</h3>
206	<p>Bumping is a way of opening standard cylinders with a specially cut key blank. It leaves almost no trace, which makes it dangerous. Anti-bump cylinders (<span lang="es">antibumping</span>) are designed so that this method doesn’t work on them.</p>
207	<p>When choosing, also look for protection against drilling (<span lang="es">antitaladro</span>), extraction (<span lang="es">antiextracción</span>) and snapping (<span lang="es">antirrotura</span>). Find out more in the section <a href="#choose">on choosing a cylinder</a>.</p>
208	<h3>How do I measure a cylinder for replacement?</h3>
209	<p>A cylinder is measured from the central fixing screw on the door edge to each end. Typical sizes are 30×30, 30×35 and 30×40 mm. The locksmith measures it on the spot and chooses the right one: the cylinder shouldn’t stick out noticeably from the door or escutcheon.</p>
210	<h3>Can I replace the lock in a rented flat?</h3>
211	<p>Yes, but it’s best to let the landlord know. Good practice is to keep the old cylinder and, when you move out, put it back or give the landlord the keys to the new one. If the lock breaks through wear, its repair is usually agreed with the landlord. Find out more in the section <a href="#documents">on documents</a>.</p>
