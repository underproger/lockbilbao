198	Which lock should I fit to my front door?
199	A good lock means a reliable mechanism, correct fitting and protection for the door’s weak points. Here’s what to look for when choosing.
200	<h3>Mortice or surface-mounted lock: which should I choose?</h3>
201	<p>A mortice lock (<span lang="es">de embutir</span>) is fitted inside the door leaf and can’t be seen from outside — it’s the main lock on most front doors. A surface-mounted lock or bolt (<span lang="es">de sobreponer</span>, <span lang="es">cerrojo</span>) is fixed to the inside of the door.</p>
202	<p>In Spain, a surface-mounted bolt is often fitted as a second lock: it makes the door harder to force and doesn’t require major changes to the door. The locksmith will advise which option suits your door.</p>
203	<h3>Do I need a second lock on my front door?</h3>
204	<p>Two locks of different designs make a door much harder to force: each needs its own method and time. If the main lock is a cylinder lock, the second is often a bolt or a lock with a different type of mechanism.</p>
205	<h3>What is a security escutcheon for?</h3>
206	<p>The cylinder is the most vulnerable part of a lock: burglars try to drill it, snap it or pull it out. A security escutcheon (<span lang="es">escudo de seguridad</span>) covers the cylinder with a steel plate and protects it against these methods.</p>
207	<p>Together with an escutcheon, it’s worth fitting an anti-bump, anti-drill cylinder. Find out more about <a href="{url:svc-cambio}">lock and cylinder replacement</a>.</p>
208	<h3>Can a lock be fitted to any door?</h3>
209	<p>Almost any door, but not any lock. A thin leaf, glass or a hollow-core door limits the choice: a large lock can’t always be morticed into them. The locksmith will inspect the door and suggest options that won’t weaken it.</p>
210	<p>If the door is misaligned, it’s adjusted first — otherwise the new lock will stick. Find out more in the section <a href="#how">on how we install locks</a>.</p>
211	<h3>What should I prepare before the locksmith’s visit?</h3>
212	<p>Photos of the door from both sides and of the edge where the current lock is, and a rough budget. If you want a particular model, tell us which — the locksmith will check whether it’s suitable. If the door is a building entrance or in a shared area, get the owners’ association’s approval beforehand. Find out more in the section <a href="#documents">on documents</a>.</p>
